Time for my first real blogpost, it seems? Despite my intention to make posts on things I have already done first, I never got around to writing those, and as this is still fresh in my mind I want to post about it first.

As I mentioned in the "Photos" tab on this website (as of the writing of this), I am quite interested in taking photos. This has basically been true since I was a child and was given a cheap camcorder but I did eventually take a class to teach me some fundamentals of photography. Despite this, I rarely ever used a dedicated camera, as I often just used the camera built into my phone. I do own a DSLR (a Canon Rebel XTi) but for me a smartphone was far more convenient. 

That being said, I have been wanting to use dedicated cameras more. A while ago I received a Canon PowerShot G10, and recently I finally wanted to try it out. 

![Picture of my Canon PowerShot G10](/images/blogposts/2026-06-27-g10-pic.png)

It is a pretty nice camera given that it is about 18 years old. It has a dedicated wheel to adjust the ISO and even exposure compensation, which is very convenient as opposed to navigating through menus. I also like the portability of it, as it has a retracting lens with 5x optical zoom. Of course there are tradeoffs – I find it not work very well in the dark. But I think that is to be expected with an old, high megapixel (14.7!) sensor in a compact design. One thing that I learned about this camera (and a lot of compact cameras like this) is the aperture setting doesn't really matter much, as the small form factor prevents any major difference between smaller and larger apertures. In fact, this camera is generally better to leave in auto mode and only really adjust the exposure compensation if desired. I would only switch to the other modes if I specifically need something (such as wanting a shadow depth of field with a wide aperture). 

When talking to a friend recently, I had also discovered that there is a resurgent appeal for "vintage" cameras, because specific older sensors have a "film" look to them. After researching this difference comes to vintage "CCD" sensors, as opposed to the modern "CMOS" sensors in all modern cameras today. The difference comes down to differing light sensing technology but in the end that means CCD sensor cameras that have not been manufactured for decades are now much more in demand, which of course means a high price to obtain. I mention this because this camera (which I happened to get for free) is one such camera with a CCD sensor. Learning this made me really interested in taking this camera on my next walks to see if I can get "film" like images. 

Well, its been a few days, and I have taken a decent amount of images. I do see the "film" like feel to the camera, although it is for certain digital. It mostly comes down to how images feel a bit softer, and how the sky looks as opposed to newer cameras. Is it enough for me to want to buy this camera if I didn't get it for free? Not really, but I do see the appeal of CCD. I don't even really care about it being like film, I just like how it looks different. Honestly I don't really think there is ever a "better" camera for photography. It is no different to how you can use crayons, markers, or MS paint to draw with; it is merely a means to an end, and it is up to you to choose the right tool to communicate your art across. I will gladly add this camera to my arsenal of cameras.

These were all the photos that I really liked from the couple of times I went for a walk. As you could probably tell, I did them all around sunset. I go for walks around this time because it becomes a really nice temperature, and seeing the sunset or night sky is always refreshing. I did edit a few of these photos to make the silhouettes darker for privacy and for enhanced effect, and I did crop others to remove buildings (it never feels right sharing pictures of peoples houses). I have also bumped the resolution to 1080px horizontally to be smaller for loading this webpage faster. Other than that, no edits were done. 

I guess thats it, I definitely plan to use this camera (and my DSLR) a lot more than I have before! You will probably see me share more pictures from this camera in my "Photos" page down the road. As for if I will be making more photography based blogposts, I will see. It will probably not be for any camera I get or picture I take but if I feel really inclined to make a post I for sure will.
